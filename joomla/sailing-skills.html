01Wind, direction, and points of sailRecognize port and starboard tack and the main points of sail.+

Port and starboard

Your forward hand is the one closest to the mast: left hand forward is port tack; right hand forward is starboard tack. Compare the hand cue with the wind direction in the illustration.

Points of sail

Use the illustration to compare the board's heading with the direction the wind comes from. For the terminology, see the Sailing Terms reference.

02Sharing the waterReview the handbook’s rules for meeting other sailors.+

The flowchart illustrates three situations between two sailing vessels. Use it with the examples below when learning to share the water.

Related: Wind, direction, and points of sail

03Carrying the board and rigCarry the equipment while keeping the sail oriented to the wind.+

Carrying the Rig shows ways to carry the board and sail and explains how to keep the sail oriented to the wind. Use Launching and landing for the CSC dock layout and approach.

04Uphauling the sailLift the sail and settle into the basic position.+

CSC dock note from 2009; review pending. The handbook describes setting the mast base beside the board’s universal and tipping the board on its side to attach the rig. Follow the Day Leader’s instructions for launching at CSC.

After moving clear of the dock, practice lifting the sail and finding a balanced starting position. Uphauling the sail illustrates the motion and explains what to do when the sail falls on the wrong side of the board.

05Starting and controlling powerMove from the basic position to sailing.+

Startup Sequence shows how to get moving after uphauling, from the balanced starting position to bringing power into the sail.

06Sailing stance and balanceKeep your stance balanced as the wind changes.+

As you begin sailing, use Stance and sail control to compare body positions and see how balance and sail control work together.

07Steering upwind and downwindUse the sail position to change the board’s direction.+

Steering illustrates how sail position changes your heading. Practice turning toward and away from the wind before working on tacks and jibes.

08Tacking: turning through the windChange direction by turning the bow into the wind.+

Tacking breaks down the turn through the wind and the transition back to sailing. Work on it after practicing steering.

09Sailing downwindPractice a run and return to a normal sailing position.+

Sailing Downwind introduces the balance and sail position of a run. Learn this after steering and tacking and before moving on to the jibe.

10The nonplaning jibeChange direction by turning away from the wind.+

Once you have practiced sailing downwind, use Nonplaning Jibe to study the footwork and sail handling for turning away from the wind.

11Building skills in higher windsExplore higher-wind sailing, harness use, water starts, and footstraps.+

Once you’re comfortable with the basics, discuss the next stage with your instructor. The 10-Step Guide has separate lessons for these skills:

  • High wind sailing: adapting stance and sail control to stronger conditions.
  • Harness: fit, line setup, and using the harness.
  • Water Starting and Footstraps: preparing for water starts and using footstraps.

12Further practice and freestyleOptional later practice after the basic skills.+

Where to go from here includes freestyle exercises for practicing board balance and rig handling. These are optional; ask your instructor which exercises suit your experience and the conditions.
